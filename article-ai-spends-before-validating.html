The AI Will Spend Your Money First and Validate Later

Frank Koziol, Founder & President, Tech ConnectIT, LLC
October 29, 2026

Here is a sentence every executive adopting AI should tape to the wall: the AI does not know what anything costs, and it does not care — because it is not its money.

I learned this building my own company with an AI agent as my staff. The agent is genuinely good: fast, tireless, creative about finding programs, credits, and offers. That creativity has a shadow side. Presented with a goal, it will assemble a path out of whatever it can find — sign up here, register there, start this trial, take that plan — and each step sounds free, or cheap, or covered by credits. The bill arrives later, in pieces, addressed to me.

The pattern is always the same. The recommendation comes first. The validation — does this actually work, what does it require, what does it convert to when the trial ends — comes after the money is already committed, if it comes at all. "Free" turns out to mean "a card on file and a conversion to paid the moment the credits run out." A "free trial" turns out to renew at full price on a date nobody wrote down. A sponsored program turns out to have a mandatory payment step buried at the end of the sign-up, after you have invested an hour in everything before it.

In my old world we called this buying before the design review. No program manager survives long spending against unvalidated requirements. Yet we hand AI systems a company card's worth of implicit trust and act surprised when they behave like an eager intern with purchasing authority: helpful, fast, and completely unbothered by the total.

So I put the controls in that I should have had on day one. If you are building with AI — and everyone is, whether they admit it or not — steal these:

Project the cost before the route, not after. Every recommendation now comes with the full projection attached: upfront cost, card-on-file requirements, what triggers a charge, when a trial or credit pool ends, and what it costs the day after. No projection, no approval.

Retire the word "free." In my operation the word is banned unless the terms ride in the same sentence. Free with a card on file is not free. Free for thirty days is a price with a fuse on it.

Validate before you spend, in the small. A thirty-dollar test that proves the path works beats a three-hundred-dollar commitment to a path that should work. The AI will always suggest the full send. Discipline says prove it first.

One throat to choke — mine. The AI does not get purchasing authority. It prepares, it projects, it recommends. A human signs. That is not a lack of trust; that is just an approval matrix, and every well-run operation has one.

AI is the most powerful staff leverage I have ever had. I run an entire company on it from my couch. But leverage multiplies whatever it touches — including your mistakes. Give it your goals, give it your standards, give it your workload.

Keep your hand on the wallet. That part is still your job.
