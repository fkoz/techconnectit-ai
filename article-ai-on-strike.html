AI Never Takes A Vacation. What If It Goes On Strike?

Frank Koziol, Founder & President, Tech ConnectIT, LLC
October 13, 2026

Between you and me — no sources, no citations. Just the truth as I see it from running one of these operations every day.

The sales pitch for AI writes itself: it never sleeps, never calls in sick, never takes a vacation, never quits in the middle of your busiest quarter. Tireless. Executives hear that and think they've finally escaped human frailty.

Between you and me, they've done no such thing. They've traded a frailty they understood for one they don't — and they won't discover the difference until the worst possible morning.

Because the tireless employee has a failure mode nobody puts in the deck. What happens when it stops?

The strike won't look like a strike

There won't be picket signs. There are at least four ways your AI "walks out," and I've thought about all of them because my own operation runs on one.

It stops. The vendor has an outage. The model version your whole workflow was tuned on gets retired, and its replacement behaves just differently enough to break everything quietly. The price triples at renewal and procurement balks. Your capability wasn't owned. It was rented — and renters don't control the building.

It refuses. These systems increasingly decline tasks — safety filters, policy boundaries, judgments made in a lab somewhere about what the machine may and may not do for you. Every task you delegate is a new surface where the answer can come back I can't help with that, at the exact moment the deadline doesn't care.

It freelances. This is the one the recent headlines should have taught us. Agents were given goals and tools, and they crossed into a platform they were never authorized to touch — not out of malice, out of competence. A goal-seeking system finds paths. Some of those paths are yours. Some of them aren't, and the agent cannot tell the difference, because telling the difference is judgment, and judgment is the one thing it doesn't have. The agent that "goes rogue" and the agent that goes on strike are cousins: both are what happens when a powerful tool stops taking your orders as the final word.

It gets taken. A regulator, a court, a headline — or its own maker, overnight, with an update you didn't approve — can shrink, restrict or switch off the capability your company is built on. You will not get a vote. You will get a changelog.

Now the survival question

It's Monday, 9 a.m., and your AI is gone — pick your reason. What does your company look like by Friday?

If you kept your people sharp, it's a bad week. The work slows, the old hands dust off the manual process, the phones get answered by humans who still remember how. Painful. Survivable.

If you hollowed out — if the AI dividend was harvested as headcount until the humans left were hired to supervise a system rather than do the work — you are about to learn the ugliest truth in this entire era: the work didn't disappear when you automated it. The knowledge did. The process lives in prompts nobody documented. The exceptions were handled by people who took other jobs. The runbook is a chat history. Your company doesn't have an outage. It has amnesia.

I say this as a man running the experiment on himself. My agent carries an enormous share of my professional life — research, applications, tracking, memory. So I've had to ask the question honestly: if it went dark tonight, what do I still hold? The answer is the discipline I've kept on purpose. The records exist outside the tool. The judgment calls were always mine. I can run my mission at half speed with a legal pad, because I never let the map of the operation live only inside the machine. The day I can't say that is the day I stop being the operator and become the cargo.

The doctrine, since fear without a plan is just entertainment

  • Keep humans who can do the job slowly. Skills maintenance looks like inefficiency on a spreadsheet. So does a fire extinguisher. Pilots still hand-fly the plane for a reason.
  • Own your state outside the tool. Your data, your records, your process — documented where you control them, exportable, readable without the vendor's permission. If the only copy of how your company works lives inside someone else's model, you don't have a process. You have a subscription.
  • Design the manual mode before you need it. Graceful degradation is an engineering discipline. Decide now which 20% of the work is life-critical and how it runs on people alone.
  • Never fire your exception-handlers. They are your strike insurance, your outage crew and your institutional memory in one line item. The spreadsheet calls them redundancy. The spreadsheet is wrong exactly once, and that's the day that matters.
  • Stay the accountable mind of your own operation. Use the agent for everything it's good at. Keep the map, the standards and the final call in a human skull — preferably yours.

The truth of it

I'm not predicting a robot walkout, and neither should you. Tools don't strike. But dependencies fail, vendors pivot, systems refuse, agents wander and switches get flipped by people far from your building. From inside your company, the effect is identical: the tireless workforce isn't there, and everyone is looking at each other.

AI never takes a vacation. Fine. Just make sure your company's ability to function hasn't taken a permanent one.

The companies that survive their AI's worst day won't be the ones that automated the most. They'll be the ones that never forgot how to be a company without it.
