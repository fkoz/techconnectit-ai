How My AI Beat My Travel Agent

Frank Koziol, Founder & President, Tech ConnectIT, LLC
October 13, 2026

A true story — and the clearest lesson I know about which work belongs to machines and which belongs to people.

Some cruise cabins aren't booked. They're hunted.

On a marquee sailing — the right ship, the right itinerary, the right week — the top suites sell out within minutes of the moment booking opens. Not days. Minutes. Miss the window and the best rooms are gone for the season, taken by people who happened to be watching at exactly the right second.

I wanted one of those rooms: an elite suite on a Holland America Alaska sailing, the Koningsdam, July 2028. The best room on the ship. And I did what any sensible operator does when the stakes are real — I built redundancy. I had a professional travel agent watching for the release. I was watching myself. Two sets of experienced eyes on the same target.

We both missed it.

Watching is not a human skill

Here's the honest truth nobody in my industry likes to say out loud: humans are terrible at monitoring. Not careless — terrible by design. We sleep. We work. We have meetings, dinners, lives. A release window that opens at a random minute on a random Tuesday does not care about any of that, and no amount of professionalism lets a person stare at a screen for weeks waiting for a door to open.

My agent didn't fail me. I didn't fail me. We were simply assigned to a job that human beings are built to lose.

My AI didn't miss it. I had set it to monitor for the opening, and the minute the cabins were announced, it caught it — before my agent saw it, before I saw it, before the crowd that takes those rooms in minutes arrived. It alerted me, the booking got done, and I got the exact suite on the exact sailing I wanted. The best room on the ship.

And here's the kicker: booking at the very front of the release came with early-bird pricing. The vigilance didn't just win the room — it saved me 20%. The machine's patience paid for a real piece of the trip.

The lesson isn't that AI is smarter

Let me be precise, because this story could be told wrong. My travel agent knows more about cruising than any AI ever will — the ships, the seasons, the tricks, the judgment. If I'd asked the AI to decide which sailing, which ship, which room was worth it, I'd have gotten a competent summary and nobody's wisdom.

That's not what happened here. Every judgment in this story was human. Mine. Which ship. Which week. Which suite. What the experience was worth to my family. The machine made none of those calls.

What the machine did was the one thing machines do better than any of us: it watched without blinking. Tirelessly, patiently, at 3 a.m. as faithfully as at noon, for as many days as it took — and it was awake in the single minute that decided everything.

That's the whole framework in one vacation: give the machine the machine-shaped work — vigilance, monitoring, speed — and keep the human-shaped work — judgment, taste, the decision itself — with the humans. Get that assignment backwards and you get the failures filling the headlines. Get it right and you get the best room on the ship at 20% off.

Every company has a cabin release

Strip away the cruise and you'll recognize your own operation in this story. Every organization sits on work that is really just watching: monitoring for the moment a price moves, a regulation posts, a competitor stumbles, a key posting opens, a risk appears. We staff that watching with people because we always have — then act surprised when the moment slips past at an hour when people were busy being people.

Stop staffing vigilance with humans. Let AI hold the watch. Keep your people for the moment the alert fires — because that's when judgment walks in, and judgment is still, stubbornly, gloriously human.

In July 2028, I'll be standing on the aft deck of the Koningsdam, watching Alaska go by from the best room on the ship — because one member of my team never sleeps, and because I knew which decisions were mine to make and which watch was never mine to hold.

My travel agent is still excellent. I just finally gave everyone on the team the job they were actually built for.
